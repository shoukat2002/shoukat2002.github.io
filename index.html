Remote Work and Wage Dynamics

Remote Work Eligibility and Wage Dynamics During the COVID-19 Pandemic: Evidence from U.S. Metropolitan Areas

Problem
Did occupations that can be performed remotely see different wage growth after the pandemic?
Approach
Difference-in-differences across 544,860 occupation-by-MSA observations from the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey for 2018, 2019, 2021 and 2022, estimated with PanelOLS using both entity and time fixed effects.
Result
In the preferred two-way fixed-effects specification, remote-eligible occupations show roughly 1.4% slower wage growth post-pandemic (p < 0.001).
The caveat
An event-study specification shows part of that gap reflects a convergence trend already underway before 2020 — so the result is a documented association, not clean causal evidence of a pandemic effect.

R OLS · Heteroskedasticity-robust SE

CPS Wage Analysis

Problem
How do gender, race, marital status, education and region relate to earnings in CPS microdata?
Approach
Three nested OLS models on log wages across 115,836 CPS observations in R, with heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors, a gender × race interaction, and regional dummies.
Result
The full specification explains 6.8% of wage variation and is jointly significant, F(8, 115827) = 1,048.5, p < 0.01.
